# Hazard-axis selection (supplemental diagnostic)

*Choice of the metrics the Hazard Filling (HF) design fills, by a pre-stated rule from a measured coverage statistic. Machinery: `src/hazard_axis_selection.py` (candidate axes, admissible sets, the pool cache, the per-selection coverage statistics, the random null, the ranking and the rule), `src/hazard_metric_image.py` (the 21-metric matrix and its pure helpers), `scripts/supplemental/hazard_axis_selection_run.py` and `hazard_axis_selection_figures.py`; wrapper `workflow/supplemental/hazard_axis_selection.sh`; settings `supplemental_config.py` (`HAXSEL_*`); tests `tests/test_hazard_axis_selection.py`. Outputs: `outputs/supplemental/hazard_axis_selection/{tables,figures}`. Selection recipe: `scenario_design_methods.md` §4.3; the candidate metrics: §3.3; the design's defining metrics: `hf_design_metrics.md`; the selector diagnostics on the adopted set: `hazard_selector_diagnostics.md`.*

---

## 1. Purpose

The HF design places Latin hypercube targets over $m$ selection axes and snaps each target to its nearest unused member of the candidate ensemble. Which metrics serve as the axes is a choice with consequences in both directions: more axes fill more facets of the hazard space directly, while each added axis weakens the snap at a fixed candidate-ensemble size, because the covering radius of any $N$-point design in $[0,1]^m$ scales as $P^{-1/m}$ (Pronzato & Müller 2012, §2.4; Beyer et al. 1999). This diagnostic makes the choice among the candidate metrics by one measured coverage statistic under pre-stated admissibility constraints, on the production candidate ensembles at the campaign $N$. It is a wrapper in the sense of Guyon & Elisseeff (2003): candidate sets are scored by running the selector itself and measuring what the delivered ensemble covers, not by a criterion computed from the metric correlation matrix.

## 2. Candidate metrics and clusters

The 21 metrics every hazard image carries (`scenario_design_methods.md` §3.3) are scored for every set: the drought run-theory metrics of the largest SSI-6 event (magnitude, duration, severity, development rate, termination rate), the drought event count and total deficit, the minimum 7-day, 12-month, 24-month and lowest-year mean flows, the flood peak discharge, pulse duration, rise rate, pulse count, days above threshold, maximum 3-day mean flow and pulse volume, the highest year mean flow, the coefficient of variation of the year means, and the Richards–Baker flashiness. The hazardous direction is high for every metric except the four low-flow minima. Two families carry the balance constraint of §3: dry-side metrics (the drought and low-flow metrics, 11) and wet-side metrics (the flood and high-flow metrics, 8); the two regime metrics belong to neither. Each metric also carries a hydrological facet label (deficit volume, intensity, rate of change, duration and frequency for droughts and low flows; peak magnitude, volume, rate of rise, duration and frequency for floods and high flows; variability for the regime metrics), the flow-regime facets of Olden & Poff (2003) by hazard, used only to read the adopted set.

**Candidate axes.** Only the continuous metrics can serve as selection axes. The Latin hypercube stratifies each axis into $N$ equal intervals and places one target in each (McKay et al. 1979), so an integer-valued metric, with far fewer attainable values than strata, cannot be filled as designed: its strata collapse onto repeated values and the target displacement on that axis is set by the value spacing rather than by the candidate ensemble. The five integer-valued metrics (drought duration in months, 53 distinct values inside the scaling bounds; event count, 6; pulse duration in days, 19; pulse count, 47; days above threshold, 148) are therefore not candidate axes; they remain scored as hazard facets. The 16 candidate axes are the remaining metrics.

**Clusters.** Metrics are grouped by average linkage on $1 - \lvert\rho_S\rvert$ cut so that pairs at $\lvert\rho_S\rvert \ge 0.7$ share a cluster, the level above which collinearity distorts statistical estimation (Dormann et al. 2013). On the production candidate ensembles the clusters are 14 and identical on both draws (table `metrics.csv`): {peak discharge, maximum 3-day flow}, {pulse duration, pulse volume}, {rise rate}, {highest year mean}, {annual CV}, {days above threshold, pulse count}, {flashiness}, {12-month minimum, lowest-year mean}, {magnitude, duration, total deficit, 24-month minimum}, {severity}, {development rate}, {termination rate}, {event count}, {7-day minimum}. Twelve of them hold a continuous metric; the decision statistic averages over those twelve.

## 3. Criteria

**Admissibility (hard constraints).** A set is admissible when (i) it holds four, five or six candidate axes; (ii) no two of its metrics have $\lvert\rho_S\rvert \ge 0.7$ on either candidate ensemble, the rule of thumb Dormann et al. (2013) confirm, so that one hazard concept cannot enter the selection distance twice under two names; and (iii) it holds at least two dry-side and two wet-side metrics, an expert constraint that keeps both hazards the operating rules respond to in the selection space (drought and flood require separate treatment; Herman et al. 2016; the facet rule of Olden & Poff 2003). On the production candidate ensembles the admissible sets number 208 of four, 826 of five and 1,421 of six metrics. The upper size is the snap-feasibility bound of the selector at $P = 10^6$: the mean target displacement is 0.05 of the scaled range at four axes and 0.23 at six against a spacing of about 0.39 between 300 targets in six dimensions, and 0.43 against 0.49 at eight, where the delivered design no longer resembles its plan (`hazard_selector_diagnostics.md`).

**The decision statistic.** The design's stated aim is coverage of the hydrologic diversity the metric set measures, with the over-representation of severe conditions a consequence of filling each axis's range uniformly rather than the objective (`scenario_design_methods.md` §4.3). The selector targets a uniform distribution on each axis's p1–p99 scaled range by construction, and the per-axis Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance to that uniform is the design's own defining coverage metric (`hf_design_metrics.md` §4; McKay et al. 1979; the one-dimensional star discrepancy of Pronzato & Müller 2012, Eq. 1; the marginal-uniformity objective of the conditioned Latin hypercube, Minasny & McBratney 2006). The decision statistic asks that quantity of every continuous metric, selected or not, in one common space that does not depend on the set. For an admissible set $S$ the selector (`lhs_nn`, $N = 300$) is run on the candidate ensemble, the selected members' values on all 16 continuous metrics are scaled by the candidate ensemble's p1–p99 bounds with clipping, $k_j(S)$ is the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance between the selected marginal of metric $j$ and the uniform distribution on $[0, 1]$, and

$$
K(S) \;=\; \frac{1}{C} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \frac{1}{\lvert c \rvert} \sum_{j \in c} k_j(S), \qquad C = 12,
$$

lower being better. Every non-redundant hazard concept counts once whatever the number of names it carries; facets the set does not select on count as much as those it does. The statistic is two-sided, so it penalizes an under-filled body and an over-filled tail alike, which a tail count cannot do; it has no quantile to choose; and because the evaluation space is fixed, sets of different size are scored on one scale. A tail-share criterion was considered and rejected on independent review: on a selected axis its value is fixed by the metric's skewness, on a non-selected metric by correlation leakage, and it rewards the hull loading that the snap produces when targets cannot be reached, so it measures distribution shift rather than coverage.

**Reported, not deciding.** The joint coverage of the candidate ensemble in the same common space, as the distance from each pool member to its nearest selected member (the minimax criterion of Johnson, Moore & Ylvisaker 1990, evaluated on the pool as Pronzato & Müller 2012 prescribe), summarized by its 99th percentile, maximum and mean on a fixed $10^5$-member i.i.d. subsample of each pool for every set and on the full pool for the leading sets; the mean and minimum edge of the minimum spanning tree of the selected members (Franco et al. 2009; Damblin et al. 2013; Bonham et al. 2024); both referenced to 200 random 300-member subsets of each pool as z-scores or ratios. The tail-share profile on all 21 metrics (the share of selected members beyond the pool p90, or below p10 for the minima; i.i.d. 0.10), the own-axis minimum tail share and attainment, the mean target displacement, the far-target share, $n_{\text{eff}}/N$ of the nearest-member weights (`hf_design_metrics.md` §5) for the leading sets, and the selected members' truncation-flag fractions.

## 4. Decision rule (pre-stated)

Admissible sets are ranked by $K$ pooled over the two candidate-ensemble draws and ten selector seeds. The leader's equivalence class is every set whose difference from the leader is smaller than two standard errors of that difference, with the standard errors from the replicate standard deviations over draws and seeds. Within the class the smallest set is adopted, and within that size the lowest estimate; this is the one-standard-error parsimony rule of statistical learning (Hastie et al. 2009, §7.10) with a two-standard-error band. The rule embeds three stated conventions and no threshold on the statistic's value: uniform on the p1–p99 range as the coverage reference (the selector's own target), equal weight per cluster (one vote per non-redundant hazard concept, by the same threshold the admissibility uses), and the two-standard-error band (a statement about replicate noise).

Three reports accompany the decision. The winner is recomputed with 16 metric votes instead of 12 cluster votes, with dry- and wet-side balanced cluster votes, and on each draw alone (table `decision_sensitivity.csv`). Draw agreement is the Spearman rank correlation of $K$ between the two candidate ensembles over all admissible sets, with the adopted set's rank on each draw alone (table `draw_agreement.csv`). The joint check (tables `leaders.csv`, `joint_check.csv`) gives the rank correlation between $K$ and the covering distance over all sets, the adopted set's z-scores against the random null, and whether any member of the equivalence class dominates the adopted set on both the covering distance and the spanning-tree mean edge.

## 5. Computation

Every admissible set is scored on the full candidate ensembles of both draws with ten selector seeds; no screening or shortlisting enters the method. Each worker process builds one scaled image per draw, every set builds one kd-tree shared by its seeds (`scengen.subsample.lhs_nn_assignment(tree=...)`), and the coverage statistics of a selection are sixteen sorts of 300 values, one nearest-member query of the $10^5$ pool subsample against the 300 members, and the spanning tree of the 300 members. Seeds are fixed per set, so the result is independent of the worker count. The nearest-member effective sample size and the full-pool covering distance are computed in a second pass for the 30 leading sets; the adopted set's members on draw 0 and the pool quantiles are written for the figures. On one workstation with 8 workers the production run took [value] minutes; the smoke configuration (`NYCOPT_HAXSEL_SMOKE=1`, the 2,000-member pools, $N = 40$) runs in seconds.

## 6. Outputs

Tables (`tables/`): `metrics.csv` (metric, family, facet, hazardous direction, integer-valued flag, candidacy, cluster per draw); `admissible_sets.csv` (set, size, members, family counts, largest within-set $\lvert\rho_S\rvert$ per draw); `wrapper_scores.csv` (one row per set, draw and seed with every scored column, `cov__{metric}` and `tail__{metric}` included); `null_scores.csv` and `null_reference.csv` (the random subsets and their mean and SD per statistic and draw); `ranking.csv` (pooled mean, standard deviation, standard error, rank, equivalence class, adopted flag and the pooled secondary statistics); `leaders.csv` (the leading sets' z-scores and ratios against the null); `joint_check.csv`; `decision_sensitivity.csv`; `draw_agreement.csv`; `adopted_members_d{k}.csv` and `pool_quantiles_d{k}.csv`; `summary.json` (the adopted set with its facets, the rule, every setting, the candidate axes, the counts and the wall time). Figures (`figures/`): `F1_decision_landscape` (every admissible set's $K$ by set size against the random-null band, the equivalence class and the adopted set; the leading sets by rank with replicate values per draw and two-standard-error whiskers); `F2_coverage_profile` (the adopted set's $k_j$ on every continuous metric and its tail share on all 21 metrics against the null band and the other leading sets, own axes filled); `F3_joint_check` ($K$ against the covering distance as null z-scores over all admissible sets); `F4_adopted_marginals` (the adopted set's selected members on every continuous metric's scaled range against the pool and the uniform target).
